# National Natural Science Foundation of China proposal support

Languages: [English](nsfc-proposal-writing.md) · [中文](nsfc-proposal-writing.zh-CN.md)

Biomed Workbench maintains separate 2026 profiles for Young Scientists Fund C, B and A, General, Regional, Key and Major programmes. It never applies US National Science Foundation criteria to an NSFC proposal or substitutes one programme's outline for another.

General and Young C proposals use the three-part 2026 body—rationale, research content and research foundation—and should in principle remain within 30 pages. The other five programme types require the current programme-specific form downloaded from the [NSFC Grants System](https://grants.nsfc.gov.cn/). If the exact form, year, programme type and file digest are not bound, the workbench does not invent a word or page limit.

The seven profiles differ in scientific emphasis: Young C focuses on an independently owned and bounded question; Young B on representative contribution, a coherent trajectory and the next research step; Young A on sustained original contribution and future leadership; General on scientific value, internal research logic and balanced feasibility; Regional on scientific merit plus locally realistic capacity; Key on limited objectives, strong foundations and a plausible breakthrough; and Major on one major scientific problem, coordinated subprojects and explicit integration. A Major project may contain no more than five subprojects under the current public programme description.

The proposal-development workflow supports composition from reviewed materials, revision of an existing draft, and hybrid rebuilding while evidence is completed. Author-supplied earlier proposals may inform general rhetorical movement, paragraph density, figure-text relationships and terminology preferences, but project-specific text, data, figures and unpublished content are not copied into another application or the public plugin.

## Chinese And English Abstracts, Followed By Full-Text Language Revision

The two abstracts and the full-text language pass are mandatory proposal deliverables, not optional polishing. They are written only after the scientific logic of the body is stable, with the Chinese abstract completed first and the English abstract built from the same scientific concepts.

The Chinese abstract normally remains within 400 Chinese characters, including punctuation; a more specific limit in the current programme form or online field takes precedence. It should form one continuous argument rather than abbreviate every section. It first defines the biological object, essential background and unresolved question; then states the testable central hypothesis and the authentic preliminary basis for entering the problem; next it compresses the interdependent aims, discriminating approach and readouts; finally it closes with the specific knowledge advance. General importance, slogans and first-use-of-a-method claims cannot replace that scientific contribution.

The English abstract is derived from the Chinese abstract without a separate length limit; it is not a word-for-word translation. It must preserve the same biological object, core phenotype, central mechanism, scientific question, hypothesis, aims, expected advance, evidence strength and uncertainty. The English version cannot strengthen causality, remove limitations or introduce a claim absent from the Chinese abstract and body.

After drafting, the complete outward-facing document is read from the title through captions and references. Engineering-development, audit-management, software-orchestration and computer-system language—such as execution engines, registries, state machines, workflow orchestration, input-output contracts, machine-readable records, renderers and hashes—is recast as biological questions, evidence, methods, comparisons and conclusions that a life-science reviewer can read naturally. Genuine bioinformatics method names, software, parameters, data structures and quantitative results remain precise. The revised document is read again for paragraph transitions, first definitions, organism-aware gene and protein nomenclature, mechanism verbs, evidence boundaries and bilingual abstract agreement; unresolved internal-process language blocks final delivery.

Once the substantive draft is stable, the proposal must pass the integrated academic voice revision in funding-proposal mode. A first pass locates formulaic openings, generic significance, unsupported priority claims, citation dumping, clause-stacked sentences and verbs stronger than the evidence. It also detects software-engineering, system-process, audit-record and computer-architecture wording that does not state a biological relation. The text is rewritten around biological entities, processes, evidence levels, experimental observations and falsifiable predictions; genuine analytical methods and parameters remain in their appropriate section. The full text is then compared with the exact source. Numbers, results, citations, technical terms, mechanism boundaries and evidence-calibrated uncertainty remain unchanged. Proposal prose is not flattened into paper-style restraint: a clear scientific vision and expected contribution remain appropriately strong when authentic preliminary evidence, prior work, a credible method or staged risk control supports them. A failed post-revision pass blocks final delivery. A passing revision is written to a navigable HTML report with evidence-file and literature links and is reopened before delivery.

Proposal literature is assembled before drafting rather than appended afterwards. A multi-source search plan covers the biological object, process, direct evidence, competing explanations and unresolved question. Papers used to establish consensus, direct support, conflict or the research gap must have their citation identity cross-checked and their relevant full text, results, figures and methods reviewed. Every source is classified as supporting, weakening, refuting, contextual or unresolved. A research gap must state the search coverage, why existing work remains insufficient and which discriminating observation the proposal will add.

Public databases are selected only when they answer a declared project question. Gene and orthology, phenotype and variant, pathway, interaction, structure, cancer-cohort, expression-atlas and trial evidence are retrieved through their corresponding adapters. Each record retains its identifier, source version or update, access date and relation to a proposal claim. Database association can support disease context, candidate prioritisation or significance, but does not by itself establish the proposed molecular mechanism. After drafting, assertion-citation coverage, citation identity, source freshness and claim-evidence integrity are checked again.

The workflow then establishes the research canon, claim-to-evidence table, scientific tension, central question, hypothesis, alternative explanations, expected knowledge advance, section jobs, aims and annual milestones. Evidence is ordered by its declared dependencies and scientific role—field premise and gap, discovery, source or context, mechanistic consistency, orthogonal validation, negative boundary and integration—rather than by upload order, method name or significance alone. Once these foundations are complete, the Codex entrypoint must continue to draft or revise the actual proposal prose; an audit report or internal writing plan is not a substitute for the requested text.

Young C, Young B and General use distinct argument and figure structures. Young C closes one independently owned, bounded question with a compact progression and direct feasibility evidence. Young B first connects representative contributions and the applicant's role into a sustained research trajectory, then derives a future leap; completed achievements and proposed work remain visually separate. General builds scientific tension from consensus and conflicting evidence, proposes a central hypothesis, and balances interdependent aims with sufficiently developed preliminary support.

Proposal figures are defined by their position in the argument before they are styled. The workbench distinguishes rationale evidence synthesis, scientific hypothesis, research workflow, technical route, preliminary foundation and achievement-lineage figures. Each figure records the preceding claims, following question, allowed conclusion and forbidden implications. Rationale figures require verified citations and full-text review. Hypothesis figures visually separate established evidence, applicant preliminary data, inference, hypothesis, alternatives and falsifying observations. Workflow and route figures show models, controls, readouts, decisions and fallbacks. Preliminary figures use real microscopy, blots, sequencing results and quantitative plots rather than generated substitutes.

Scientifically meaningful tissue architecture, cell states, subcellular localisation, molecular assemblies, model systems, experimental setups and observed-data panels carry the majority of the visual narrative across at least two relevant scales. Labels remain concise supporting elements; a decorated boxes-and-arrows outline is not accepted as a scientific proposal figure. Image generation is limited to a non-evidentiary visual reference or separable biological assets. Exact labels, citations, connectors, legends and quantitative panels are reconstructed as native editable objects. Final delivery includes editable PPTX, PDF and high-resolution PNG.

Every iteration is reviewed at the final insertion size. One or two font families, a restrained size hierarchy and a small text-colour set are used consistently. Labels, legends and data remain above backgrounds, biological assets and connectors in the declared layer order. Logically corresponding elements retain matched horizontal or vertical positions; text cannot cover a scientific visual, leave its intended boundary or be hidden by a lower-level element. Key scientific elements require concise labels unless a specific exception is justified. Each element is reviewed backwards from its scientific contribution and narrative weight so that repeated or meaningless decoration is removed, while regional information density and whitespace remain balanced. In Word, figure width cannot exceed the document text area; the figure occupies its own explicitly centred paragraph with no leading spaces, tabs, empty runs or indentation placeholders. Review also covers biological accuracy, arrow meaning, evidence colours, source attribution, caption agreement and A4 page fit. Targeted corrections are rendered again until both scientific-semantic and layout error lists are empty.

The semantic review checks section ownership, title-body congruence, paragraph transitions, entity introduction order, Chinese-English abstract concepts, authentic preliminary evidence, and aim-to-phenotype-to-mechanism relevance. It also detects untethered broad surveys, unresolved ordering between global and conditional genetic models, duplicated research-content and technical-plan claims, and annual plans that do not map to aims. A mechanism-claim review prevents co-expression or colocalisation from being written as direct substrate or causal evidence. The terminology review checks first definitions, abbreviations, gene-protein distinctions, organism-aware symbol styling and natural Chinese scientific prose.

The DOCX delivery review reads native Word packages, checks numbered citations in first-appearance order, normalises citation separators and ranges, enforces citation-reference reciprocity, compares typography against a policy derived from the current official form, and checks figure and table caption sequences. Page-level rendered review covers clipping, overlap, legibility, final figure size, page breaks and whether captions remain with their figures. When citation markers are not split across Word runs and the reference list is complete and unique, it can create a separate automatically renumbered Word copy. It never silently overwrites the source, and the derived copy must be audited again.
