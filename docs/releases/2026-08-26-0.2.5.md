# 0.2.5 · 2026-08-26

[English](2026-08-26-0.2.5.md) · [中文](2026-08-26-0.2.5.zh-CN.md) · [Release index](README.md)

This release shifts development from adding method names toward correct selection for complex research tasks, observed-result closure, and publication-ready delivery. The registry now contains 215 scientific modules, with engineering validation, method applicability, and project-level claim boundaries kept distinct.

## complex scientific semantics and project governance

- Routing first resolves assay, target, control, normalisation and biological relation, including negative constraints and terminology disambiguation. Complex multi-omics requests retain multiple scientific branches and a final integration node.
- Minimal-sufficient analysis defaults to one primary analysis plus one orthogonal validation or sensitivity analysis. A new method must state what it replaces and which decision information it adds.
- Project-level configuration fixes the sample sheet, genome version, cell annotation, statistical unit, thresholds, colours and formal directories, while separating formal, candidate, sensitivity and deprecated results.
- User-facing reports prioritise conclusions, effect sizes, evidence boundaries and the next decision; detailed provenance, digests and state records remain in the supporting evidence files.

## RNA processing and alternative splicing

A dedicated RNA processing and alternative splicing module now covers splice events, exon usage, transcript usage, intron retention, 3′ bias and isoform evidence, including input qualification, method selection, statistical units, quality review and claim boundaries. It separates RNA-processing descriptions supported by snRNA or 3′ data from alternative-splicing claims that require appropriate junction, transcript or long-read coverage, and does not treat intronic versus exonic signal as direct splicing evidence.

## National Natural Science Foundation of China proposals

- Drafting requires multi-source literature discovery, landscape review, full-text reading of central sources, citation-identity resolution, resolver adjudication, claim-evidence review and project-relevant public-database retrieval.
- Young C, Young B, Young A, General, Regional, Key and Major programmes use distinct argument priorities. Young C focuses on one independently owned bounded question; Young B derives a future research leap from representative contributions and the applicant's role; General balances evidence tension, a central hypothesis, interdependent aims and sufficiently developed preliminary support.
- Rationale, scientific-hypothesis, research-workflow, technical-route, preliminary-foundation and achievement-lineage figures have separate content contracts. Tissue, cellular, subcellular, molecular, model-system and observed-data elements must carry the visual narrative; text boxes and connectors alone cannot pass.
- Image generation is limited to a non-evidentiary visual reference or separable biological assets. Exact labels, citations, semantic connectors, legends and quantitative panels are rebuilt as editable objects. Delivery includes PPTX, PDF and high-resolution PNG, with scientific and layout review repeated at final insertion size until no error remains.
- Figure acceptance also freezes typography and text colours, z-order, correspondence alignment, text boundaries, label completeness, element narrative weights and regional information density, preventing occlusion, overflow, redundant decoration and locally crowded or empty compositions.
- Word delivery also checks that figure width stays within the text area and that the figure paragraph is explicitly centred without leading spaces, tabs, empty runs or indentation placeholders.

## Validation scope

Proposal drafting, figure prompting, editable-reconstruction contracts and RNA-processing modules use controlled synthetic cases that contain no private research content. Registration or a passing controlled case does not establish a scientific result for an arbitrary project; project use must bind actual literature, database records, source or analysis artifacts, and final visual review.
