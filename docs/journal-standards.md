# Journal Positioning And Manuscript Requirements

Biomed Workbench matches a project to journals by scientific remit, intended readership, article type, study design, evidence maturity and translational reach. The result is a reasoned shortlist: each recommendation states why the project fits, where it is weaker, and which evidence or manuscript changes would materially improve the match.

## What The Assessment Covers

- journal audience, scientific scope and preferred article families;
- compatibility between the study design, mechanistic depth, validation level and the journal's editorial remit;
- required manuscript sections, declarations and reporting checklists;
- article-type limits for the abstract, main text, references and display items when an exact limit applies;
- figure composition, source-data expectations, image integrity and accessibility;
- data, code, resource, ethics, trial-registration and conflict-of-interest statements where relevant;
- language style, title and abstract framing, and the distinction between observation, association, mechanism and causality.

## How To Use It

Provide the project question, study design, principal findings, validation layers, intended audience and any constraints on article type or publication route. If a manuscript already exists, provide its section list, word counts, figures, tables, declarations and reporting checklists. The workbench returns journal-fit reasoning, an article-type recommendation, a field-level compliance review and a prioritized revision plan.

Requirements are bound to the selected journal profile and its review date. A new assessment is required when the target journal, article type or author instructions change. Missing or ambiguous requirements remain explicit checks and prevent a submission-ready conclusion until resolved.
